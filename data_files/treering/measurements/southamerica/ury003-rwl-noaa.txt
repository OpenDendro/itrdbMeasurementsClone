#   Abstract: Exotic invasive species display rapid growth rates and growth dynamics that contribute to the invasion process throughout the lifetime of the species. Gleditsia triacanthos is an invasive species in 43 countries and threatens diversity and ecosystem services in riparian environments. Here we evaluate the growth dynamics and climate sensitivity of Gleditsia triacanthos, a major ecological concern in a protected riparian wetland of the Uruguay River in subtropical South America. Trees ranged in age from 6-55 y old, demonstrating its long-term establishment in the 1970s and persistence in the study area. Growth declines in diameter and volume at 7-10 y of age suggested potential tradeoffs that resulted in a slowing of otherwise explosive growth rates at juvenile and subadult stages of 20-25 mm y-1.  The spatial distribution of tree ages demonstrate a north-south colonization process in areas of deposition and succession within the protected area. Growth release events occurred in years 2000, 2009, and 2012-2014. Annual growth was favored by warm springs and spring to early summer precipitation, particularly for age classes of 6 to 20 y old. Meanwhile, results suggest that river level may favor growth up to a threshold, but exceptional flooding can hinder growth, particularly as trees age. Overall, these results provide insight as to the ecology and lifetime growth dynamics of this invasive species. It also complements current information on dispersal and recruitment strategies with multidecadal growth dynamics from 1970-2023,  demonstrating how hydroclimate and ontogeny mediate the invasion process.

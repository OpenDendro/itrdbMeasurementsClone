#   Description: The data were collected to describe stand age dynamics in silver maple dominated forest stands in navigation pools 3 through 10 of the Upper Mississippi River. The data may be used to assess tree breast height age and relationships between tree size and age within the northern floodplain forests of the UMR. Annual ring-width data for silver maple trees sampled in this project are available in separate data releases (e.g., doi.org/10.25921/z8ke-d754).

#   Abstract: Forest community age and growth datasets are fundamental building blocks of stand development models critical for effective management. In ecosystems such as the northern Upper Mississippi River (UMR) floodplain, there is little to no existing research quantifying tree age dynamics, especially using meticulous dendrochronological methods. To fill this data gap, we analyzed increment cores from 850 overstory trees across 13 sites in the northern UMR, producing novel information on floodplain forest recruitment timelines and size-age relationships within the region. At the landscape level, multiple dominant species showed peak recruitment around the 1930-50s. Mean stand breast height age ranged from 54 to 80 years, with high variability in the range and shape of age distributions at site and plot scales. Overstory recruitment began to decline beginning in the 1980s. Five of the seven most abundant species in our sample had linear diameter-age relationships, with high residual variability in some species. Our findings highlight potential roles of both regional and local drivers in the development of the present-day floodplain forest, creating a mosaic of age structures and successional processes within the silver maple (Acer saccharinum L.) dominated forest type.
